What the chart shows

Across: air temperature (50–100°F). Up: relative humidity (30–95%). Colour and the labelled isotherms: how hot it feels to a runner at the pace on the slider, in still air. The “now” dot is the current temperature and humidity in New York City (from Open-Meteo); if it falls outside the box it sits clamped at the edge.

Why running changes the answer

Ordinary “feels like” formulas, like the Steadman apparent temperature used here as the base, assume a body at rest — they have no input for how hard you’re working. A working muscle puts out 5–15× resting heat, and in still air that extra heat has to leave almost entirely by sweat evaporation, which humidity throttles. So the faster you run and the muggier it is, the further the “feels like” temperature climbs above the resting value.

The runner and the slider

  • Runner: 160 lb, 5′10″ (height doesn’t enter — the equations scale with body mass only).
  • MET (metabolic equivalent): energy use as a multiple of resting. 1 MET is sitting still (≈1 kcal per kg per hour, about 88 W for this runner); a 9:00 /mi run is ≈11 MET. The pace caption shows both MET and watts (W), the heat output that drives the “feels like” shift.
  • Pace: 6:00–12:00 /mi in 15-second steps, then one final stop: a 20:00 /mi gentle walk. That walk is really ≈3.3 MET / ≈290 W, about 3× resting, but is treated as no exertion: at that output, radiation and convection carry the extra heat even in calm air.
  • Wind: ambient wind is fixed at 0 mph (worst case). The checkbox lets the runner’s own ground speed count as a headwind, since moving through calm air creates the same relative airflow a breeze would.

How it’s computed

  • Base “feels like” (Steadman / Australian BOM): AT = T + 0.33·e − 0.70·v − 4.0 (°C), e = vapour pressure from T and RH, v = wind in m/s (0, plus the runner’s speed if the checkbox is on).
  • Pace → watts (ACSM running equation, level ground): speed = 1 mi / pace; VO2 = 0.2·speed(m/min) + 3.5 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹; METs = VO2/3.5; watts = METs × 3.5 × 72.6 kg ÷ 200 × 69.78.
  • Exertion offset: ΔAT = k × (0.35 + 0.65·RH/100) × 1/(1 + 0.06·vmph) °F, with k = max(0, (watts − 100)/24). The humidity factor is evaporative cooling failing as air nears saturation; the last factor is airflow partly relieving that, with diminishing returns.

Caveats

  • The exertion offset is a rule-of-thumb shift, not a solved heat-balance model (that would be WBGT / ISO 7933). Treat all magnitudes as illustrative, not medical guidance.
  • This is one of several “feels like” formulas. The US NWS instead switches between a Heat Index (T + RH only) and Wind Chill (T + wind only), never a true 3-D field.
  • The box is sized for NYC summers. Its hot, humid corner (100°F at 95% RH) is rarer than it looks: local dew points seldom pass the upper 70s°F.
  • No external libraries; everything is computed and drawn in the page.
